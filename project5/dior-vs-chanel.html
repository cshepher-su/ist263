Chanel and Dior Play Two Different Games

Head-to-Head

Chanel

What Makes Chanel's Strategy Work

Chanel's whole approach comes down to one idea, which is that consistency beats visibility. The brand would rather be seen less often and remembered more clearly. Its marketing favors quality impressions over volume. It picks placements that build cultural relevance and artistic credibility instead of chasing reach (Latterly). That discipline protects Chanel's pricing power. A brand people see everywhere starts to feel less exclusive. But the tradeoff is real. Chanel's campaigns still lean on legacy and elegance. The brand "still appears cautious" when it comes to connecting with Gen Z audiences (Digital Agency Network).

Dior

How Dior Does It Differently

Dior takes almost the opposite approach. The brand shows up everywhere, on every major social platform, in print magazine ads, and through virtual exhibitions. Dior even segments its audience by running separate accounts like Dior Homme for menswear (StartupTalky). Chanel treats restraint as a feature, while Dior treats visibility as one. Dior regularly collaborates with contemporary artists, including a partnership with artist Daniel Arsham that reimagined the house's classic saddle bag, to keep the brand feeling current for younger consumers (Marketing Explainers). That strategy seems to work, at least by one measure. A 2023 Luxe Digital study found Dior captured 13.83% of total online search interest among luxury brands. That put Dior ahead of Gucci, Chanel, and Louis Vuitton, the next three brands on the list (Luxe Digital, via Fashion Dive).

So, Which Approach Wins?

These two brands aren't playing the same game. Dior's broader digital footprint wins more search traffic and keeps the brand feeling fresh with younger shoppers. Chanel's restraint is what keeps the brand feeling untouchable, even if it means moving slower with Gen Z. Chanel still grew revenue 16% in 2023 without chasing search rankings the way Dior does, so restraint clearly hasn't hurt its bottom line. In luxury, being everywhere and being unforgettable aren't the same thing. Chanel bets its entire strategy on the second one.

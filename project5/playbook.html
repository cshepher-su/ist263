Inside Chanel's PR Playbook

Runway Shows as PR Events

For Chanel, a runway show isn't just a collection presentation. It's a media event built to dominate the news cycle. Under Karl Lagerfeld, Chanel built full scale sets inside the Grand Palais in Paris, ranging from an airport terminal, to a supermarket, to a rocket launch pad (Grow Your Clothing Brand). These sets weren't just for the people in the room. They generated headlines and social content that spread the collection far beyond those who attended.

The Métiers d'Art Traveling Show

Since 2002, Chanel has staged an annual Métiers d'Art show. The show celebrates the specialty ateliers Chanel owns, the embroiderers, feather workers, milliners, and shoemakers who still do this work by hand (WWD). Two years after the first show, Chanel started staging it in a different world city each year. Karl Lagerfeld took it on a global tour that hit New York, Monte Carlo, London, Moscow, Shanghai, Bombay, and Edinburgh (Her World). More recent stops include the Temple of Dendur at the Met in 2018, the Château de Chenonceau in France, Dakar in Senegal, which marked Chanel's first show in Africa, plus Hangzhou and Manchester (WWD, Maison De Chanel). For 2026, new creative director Matthieu Blazy staged the show inside a subway station in New York that the city had shut down years earlier. Guests included A$AP Rocky, Margaret Qualley, Tilda Swinton, Kristen Stewart, Solange, G Dragon, and Charlotte Casiraghi (WWD). The next stop is Rome. Coco Chanel had a personal history with the city that spans decades (WWD). Every location ties back to a piece of the brand's history. The show works as both a fashion event and a heritage marketing tool.

The Ambassador System

Chanel celebrity endorsement deals function more so as a way of inducting people into a lineage. Margot Robbie became a Chanel ambassador in 2018. She became the face of Chanel No. 5 in 2024, joining a line of past faces that includes Catherine Deneuve and Nicole Kidman (WWD). In 2023, Whitney Peak became the first Black ambassador for Coco Mademoiselle. She followed Kate Moss and Keira Knightley in the role (Wikipedia). Recent campaigns also feature Timothée Chalamet as the face of Bleu de Chanel. JENNIE and Gracie Abrams starred together in the Coco Crush jewelry campaign, and Kylie Minogue made a cameo in the CHANEL 25 handbag campaign (Acting Magazine). None of these counts as advertising in the traditional sense of buying space to push a product. It works more like casting. Chanel pairs the right cultural figure with the right product, and the campaign gets covered as entertainment news instead of marketing.
